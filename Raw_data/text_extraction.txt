Digitizing Wilson 1932

Information about each site is found in Appendix A (page 534). This information will be used to populate the 'site_data' file. 
The columns are as follows: 
site - the given site name. This will be used to link the species occurrence data to locations
region - the general area (e.g. Woods Hole, Falmouth, Cape Cod, etc.)
description - the description provided in the text (should be copied exactly)
salinity - the general salinity category (fresh, brackish, salt) from the description
lat - the inferred latitude of the pond
long - the inferred longitude of the pond
notes - other useful notes or comments about this site

Information from the species descriptions will populate the 'species_occurrence' file. 
There are only three columns in this file: 
species - the given species name (note: these may have been changed since the publication of this book; corrections handled programmatically at a later time)
updated species name - images in the Wilson book were cross-referenced with more recent images; species names were updated
site - the locations listed in the Occurrence section of each species description 

Note: several of the species were collected from sites not listed in Appendix A. In these cases, you should add the sites to the 'site_data' file. In the notes column, include the species/page the location was mentioned

Protocol: 

- species of interest was recorded in the 'species_occurance' file in the 'species' column
- the occurrence of said species was copied from the Wilson book into the 'site' column
- if a species occurs at a site not listed in Appendix A, that site was added to the 'site_data' file
	- the region, subregion, salinity, and species name and page number where the site was found was also added
- these steps were repeated for every species

Climate Affinity for Wilson Species

Information about the geographic range of species from the Wilson book will be in the 'range_distributions' file. The columns are as follows:
updated_species - a direct copy of the 'updated_species' column from the 'species_occurance' file
range_classification - depending on the lowest latitude of the range, each species is classified as C (cold), T (temperate), or W (warm
range_description - the geographic bounds of the distribution; range of high to low latitude (e.g. Canada to Mexico, Canada to Kentucky, etc.)
low_lat_limit - the lowest latitude where the species was described
high_lat_limit - the highest latitude where the species was described

Protocol:

- for every species in the 'updated_species' column in the 'range_distributions' file, several sources were cross referenced to find the most accurate distribution. These sources were:
	- Wilson, M.S. & H.C. Yeatman. 1959. Free-Living Copepoda. [in] W.T. Edmondson (ed.). Ward & Whipple Fresh-Water Biology, 2nd edition
	- Reid, J.W. & C.E. Williamson, Copepoda. [in] J.H. Thorp & A.P. Covich (eds.). Ecology and Classification of North American Freshwater Invertebrates, 3rd edition: 829-899.
	- “GBIF.” n.d. Accessed August 17, 2026. https://www.gbif.org/. (note: GBIF was only used when the occurrence shown was either DNA sequenced or a preserved specimen from the Smithsonian records)
- From these sources, the 'low_lat_limit' and 'high_lat_limit' were recorded, and used to determine the 'range_distibution' of each species
	- the relevant sources where each data point came from were also recorded
- using the information from the 'range_distibution' column, each species was classified as either C (cold), T (temperate), or W (warm):
	- cold: lowest latitude limit from Canada to within New England
	- temperate: lowest latitude limit from below New England to North Carolina
	- warm: lowest latitude limit below North Carolina
